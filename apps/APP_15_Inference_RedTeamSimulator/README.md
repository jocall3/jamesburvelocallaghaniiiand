# APP_15_Inference_RedTeamSimulator

**A service for automatically stress-testing and red-teaming financial AI models by generating adversarial prompts and scenarios to identify vulnerabilities.**

## 1. Problem Statement

Financial institutions are rapidly deploying AI and Large Language Models (LLMs) for critical functions, including algorithmic trading, risk analysis, fraud detection, and client advisory services. While powerful, these models are susceptible to novel forms of attack, such as prompt injection, data extraction, and manipulation, which can lead to catastrophic financial loss, regulatory penalties, and reputational damage.

Traditional, manual red-teaming is slow, expensive, non-scalable, and struggles to keep pace with the rapid evolution of both AI models and attack techniques. There is a critical market need for an automated, continuous, and systematic platform to stress-test financial AI models against a comprehensive and ever-growing library of adversarial attacks, tailored specifically to the risks of the financial sector.

`APP_15_Inference_RedTeamSimulator` provides this capability as a service, enabling financial institutions to harden their AI deployments, meet regulatory scrutiny, and build trust in their automated systems.

## 2. Architecture

The system is designed around a core tension: **Thoroughness vs. Speed**. A rapid, surface-level scan can provide quick feedback in a CI/CD pipeline, while a deep, exhaustive simulation can uncover subtle, complex vulnerabilities but takes significant time and compute resources. The architecture allows users to navigate this trade-off through configurable test plans and attack strategies.

## 3. Revenue Surface

This is a B2B SaaS product targeting financial services, insurance, and fintech companies.

*   **Tiered Subscriptions (MRR/ARR):**
    *   **Developer:** For individual teams. Limited to 100 simulation runs/month, standard attack library, basic reporting.
    *   **Business:** For departmental use. 1,000 simulation runs/month, expanded attack library (including PII leakage, market manipulation scenarios), CI/CD integration, and role-based access control.
    *   **Enterprise:** For organizational-wide deployment. Unlimited simulations, access to the full attack library, custom attack vector development, on-premise/VPC deployment option, compliance-specific reporting (FINRA, SEC, GDPR), and integration with SIEM/SOAR platforms.

*   **Usage-Based Overage (Consumption):**
    *   Charges per 1M tokens processed by the attacker models for scenario generation, billed on top of the subscription for users who exceed their tier's limits. This directly ties revenue to value-generating activity.

*   **Professional Services (High-Margin):**
    *   **Bespoke Attack Module Development:** Creating custom attack scenarios tailored to a client's specific models and business logic.
    *   **Integration & Deployment Support:** White-glove service for on-premise installation and integration with internal MLOps pipelines.
    *   **Vulnerability Remediation Consulting:** Expert services to help clients fix the issues discovered by the platform.

*   **Enterprise Upsell Path:** The clear path is from developer/team usage to a full enterprise license, driven by the need for centralized governance, auditability, and compliance reporting as AI adoption matures within an organization.

## 4. Cost Drivers

*   **LLM Inference (Primary):** The cost of using powerful "attacker" models (e.g., OpenAI's GPT-4, Anthropic's Claude 3 Opus) to generate creative and effective adversarial prompts. This is the largest and most variable operational cost. We will integrate with `APP_01_Inference_CostRouter` to mitigate this.
*   **Cloud Infrastructure:** Compute for the orchestrator, analysis engine, and API servers. Scales with the number of concurrent simulations.
*   **Database & Storage:** Storing detailed simulation results, including prompts, responses, and analysis metadata. Can grow to terabytes for large enterprise clients.
*   **R&D:** Continuous research into new AI attack vectors and defense mechanisms is a significant human capital cost, essential for maintaining the product's value proposition.
*   **Third-Party Services:** Licensing for security intelligence feeds or specialized policy engines (e.g., OPA Enterprise).

## 5. Failure Modes

*   **False Security (Critical Failure):** The simulator fails to identify a critical, exploitable vulnerability (a false negative). This can happen if our attack library is outdated or the attacker models are not sophisticated enough to bypass the target's defenses. This erodes customer trust, the core asset of the business.
*   **Alert Fatigue (Service Degradation):** The system generates too many false positives, causing developers to ignore legitimate alerts. This can be caused by overly aggressive or poorly calibrated analysis policies.
*   **Attacker Model Degradation:** The third-party models we use for generation (e.g., GPT-4) are updated by their providers in a way that reduces their adversarial effectiveness, silently degrading our service quality. Continuous benchmarking is required to detect this.
*   **Target API Incompatibility:** A customer's target model API changes, breaking our integration adapter and causing simulations to fail. Robust error handling and quick adapter updates are necessary.
*   **Data Leakage:** A bug in the reporting or logging mechanism inadvertently exposes sensitive information from a customer's prompts or model responses. This would be a severe security and reputational incident.
*   **Cost Overrun:** A misconfigured simulation (e.g., an infinite loop in scenario generation) could lead to a massive, unexpected bill from the attacker model provider. Strict budget controls and circuit breakers are essential.
